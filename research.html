Generative modeling for IDP ensembles

Intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) do not fold into a dominant structure, instead they sample an ensemble of conformations, and that ensemble is what reveals their function. We built a tool called idpSAM+ that generates these conformational ensembles for IDPs and IDRs in just a couple of minutes. idpSAM+ works by first training a latent diffusion model to learn the geometry of the IDP backbone (Cα atoms) in a compact latent representation. From that representation, the model learns the sequence-conditioned distribution of conformations. At inference time, idpSAM+ generates realistic conformational ensembles directly from a sequence.

Biophysics-based variant effect predictor

Understanding how mutations affect protein function is crucial for genetic disease, and for protein evolution, engineering, and design. However, the sequence-to-function relationship is highly complex, involving multi-site, high-dimensional relationships and dynamics. Several variant effect predictors exist to learn this relationship; however, it is challenging to achieve good performance under extrapolation. This work shows that incorporating protein interactions, dynamics, and physical energetics directly into convolution-based approaches can significantly improve extrapolation performance.

HyRes-IDRome: A Large-Scale Simulation Dataset of Intrinsically Disordered Proteins

We have developed a dataset for IDP ensemble. This dataset contains conformational ensembles generated by HyRes physics-based molecular dynamics simulations, together with selected sequence properties and simulation-derived observables, for intrinsically disordered proteins and regions (IDPs/IDRs) from the human proteome in UniProt database and the DisProt database.
