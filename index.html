Working at the intersection of biophysics and machine learning

I am a PhD candidate in Computational Biophysics at University of Massachusetts Amherst. I am studying the biomolecules by utilizing machine learning and biophysics based methods. Specifically, my work combines molecular modeling, molecular dynamics, and machine learning to study intrinsically disordered proteins and their ensembles. Also, how the mutations in a protein sequence can perturbe its function.

Currently, I working under the guidance of Prof. Jianhan Chen. By working in Chen's lab, I have gained extensive experience in molecular dynamics simulations of both structured and disordered proteins, variant effect predictors, generative models for proteins conformational ensembles, large and extensive dataset development and management. Also, I got the opportunity to collaborate with many resechers from different fields and resulted in the successful projects. Additionally, I got the opportunity to present my research in several platforms and conferences.

Previously, I have completed my integrated masters from National Institute of Science Education and Research (NISER) Bhubaneswar, India under the guidance of Prof. Upakarasamy Lourderaj in 2022. Here, I worked on developing traditional machine learing models to represent potential energy surfaces of 3-dimensional systems including small molecules, and models for the predicting the outcome from initial postions and velocities for enhancing the sampling for the rare states.

Looking ahead, I am currently seeking industry positions, particularly in biopharma, biotech, and AI-driven drug discovery that require in-depth knowledge of machine learning, molecular dynamics simulations, molecular modeling, and data science.
